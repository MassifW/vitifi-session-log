# 2026-10-30 11:06 — VITIFI as an installed app: cost, benefits and pitfalls, looked at again

Mike: "let's take another look at turning Vitifi into an installed app. What would be the cost, benefits and pitfalls of doing this?" It last came up on 27 Oct, as the only way to record a track with the screen locked (`docs/decisions.md`, "Tracks when the screen locks, and row metres"). Nothing built; no code or database change. Written into that section of `docs/decisions.md` and the matching item in `docs/open-items.md`.

**Money (searched 30 Oct; third-party pages, not Apple's or Google's own):** Apple's developer programme US$99 a year, paid in NZ$ at enrolment; an organisation needs a D-U-N-S number (free, said to take up to 30 days) and a website. Google Play US$25 once; a personal account made after Nov 2023 must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before it may publish, an organisation account does not. Background location: a free maintained plugin (Capgo's fork), or Transistorsoft's at about US$399 once per app, a figure from a competitor's blog, not confirmed. Netlify and Supabase unchanged: the shell loads the same site, and a web release still costs its 15 credits.

**What it would give beyond the Home Screen app:** recording with the screen locked (the reason it came up); its own mobile-data and location switches in Settings (the Home Screen app uses Safari's, which was the "no 5G" fault); a new icon arrives with an update instead of remove-and-add; a store listing for other wineries; later, Bluetooth instruments and NFC on an iPhone, which Safari does not offer a web page at all. **What it would not:** speed (the time is the round trip to Sydney), GPS precision (same chip), notifications (a Home Screen app on iOS 16.4 or later can have them already).

**Pitfalls particular to VITIFI:**
- It runs on server actions and middleware, so it cannot be packed into the app as files; the shell loads https://vitifi.netlify.app. It needs signal exactly as now.
- On an iPhone a shell (WKWebView) runs a service worker only for domains listed as App-Bound Domains, and a WebKit bug report says turning those on can break the script injection Capacitor's plugins depend on. So the no-signal opening and native background GPS in one shell is unproven. Xcode installs onto one's own iPhone with a free Apple ID (the install lasts 7 days), so this can be tried before any money is spent.
- The shell keeps its own stored copies, apart from the Home Screen app's: anything waiting to be sent must be sent before changing over, and everyone signs in again.
- Invitation and reset links open in Safari unless universal links (`apple-app-site-association`, Android's `assetlinks.json`) are served; the sign-in-link handling would need trying again inside the shell.
- Apple's review (guidelines read 30 Oct): 2.1 wants a working demo login showing the full app, and none can be given with real winery data, so a demo winery with made-up records first; a privacy policy (none yet; it is on the suggested list); 4.2 refuses a "repackaged website", and background location is the native part that answers it; 5.1.1(v) account deletion applies only once accounts can be created in the app (today they are by invitation); 3.1.3(c) lets VITIFI be sold to wineries outside the app, with no buy button inside it.
- Web releases go on as now with no review; a shell release goes through each store's review. An old shell meeting new web code has to be expected: the page checks whether the native recorder is there and falls back to the web one, and still works in a plain browser.
- Both stores want the shell rebuilt yearly for their newest SDK whether or not anything changed, and lapsed membership takes the listing down.
- Whose account it is (Mike, the winery, or Ampel Lab Limited) belongs with the IP paper; moving a listing between accounts later is possible but fiddly.

**Rough effort (my estimate, not measured):** the shell, the native recorder feeding `app/lib/trackRecording.tsx`, and universal links, about three to five sessions plus field tries; the listing (privacy policy, demo winery, screenshots, review rounds) two to four more, and two to six weeks of calendar time for D-U-N-S and review.

**Suggested order:** the field try of the released recorder (already the open item); if it falls short on a real spray round, the free trial shell on Mike's iPhone, for the two unknowns above; if both work, a private install for the staff (TestFlight, and an Android file or Play internal testing), US$99 a year; a public listing when a second winery is signing up, under whichever company owns VITIFI by then.

**Uncertain:** every price and store rule above is from secondary pages on 30 Oct; the App-Bound Domains conflict is from a bug report and forum posts, not tried; the effort is a guess.
