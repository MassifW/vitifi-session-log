# 1 Nov 2026 — Stock take differences, for the customs adjustment

**Asked (Mike, the day of a stock take):** after each stock take the discrepancies were totalled as litres by hand for the customs adjustment entered in New Zealand's Trade Single Window. He wants that record in the app, each discrepancy with a reason that can be added and edited later, and likely causes suggested. Printable or downloadable, only the wines that differ, "nothing differs" kept apart from "could not check".

**Done** (on a branch, PR #46; its database part waits for Mike's yes):
- **The record:** Stock, "Stock take differences": each wine counted whose count differs from the records, counted and expected, in bottles and in litres by bottle size, and the total in litres up or down for the adjustment. It prints on its own and downloads as a spreadsheet.
- **Against the records as they stand now,** in the same order the database uses for stock on hand (day, then when each line was entered). A sale dated before the count and entered after it counts as gone; otherwise a missing sale found later would be counted twice, once in the excise month and once as a stock take loss. The figure at the count is shown beside it, so a figure already lodged can be reconciled.
- **A note on each line,** kept in a small new table, one per wine, place and day, so it survives a correction of the stock take or a second count the same day. Row-level security: everyone reads, whoever may take stock writes. In "All records" and the nightly Drive copy.
- **Places to look,** suggestions only: another wine of the same size out by the opposite amount, a sign-out of the same number since the last count or just after the count, a set-aside of that number, a bottling since the last count, nothing signed out at all, and for owners a Xero invoice of that number with no sign-out near it.

**Checked:** on a local copy of the night's backup: the database harness and the full test suite pass, every database suite ran; in a browser as an owner and as a read-only login, including a note read back after a reload and a back-dated sale moving a line.
