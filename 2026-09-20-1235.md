# A vessel to measure, an area corrected, and the grid-versus-vines audit

*Volumes, yields, vine counts, block layouts and input rates are withheld
from this public copy.*

## 1. A vessel that needs a tape measure

Recorded at one capacity; the components pressed into it during one
vintage sum to considerably more. Either that line over-claims for that
press run, or the vessel is mis-recorded. The record cannot separate the
two, so **neither is applied** — it is logged as a data-quality item with
what to do if it measures larger: correct the vessel, supersede the
derived rows, and re-derive every vintage it has held.

Correcting it on a guess would move a vintage's recovery figure, which is
exactly the kind of number that gets quoted.

## 2. An area corrected, and its blast radius

The pergola's area now follows the grid rather than the planted count.
Nothing derived changes today, and the reason matters more than the
change:

- The planting's area field is **carried, never calculated with** — yield
  per hectare comes from vines and density, not from area.
- **No harvest pick has ever resolved to the pergola**, and there are no
  pick lines at all, so no yield figure has ever existed for it.
- The landing screen's totals read a separate, older layer.
- It had been quoted only in my own session logs, this session.

Corrected before anything depended on it, which is the only comfortable
time.

**A new inconsistency found in that older layer, and deliberately not
fixed.** Its pergola row counts grid spaces — correctly — while recording
an area derived from the planted figure, and the erroneous one at that.
Count and area disagree about which question they answer. Logged rather
than overwritten, because that layer's areas come from several sources
including survey, and changing one on arithmetic alone would be guessing
at provenance.

## 3. The audit

I had meant to fan this out; the agents all hit a spend limit and
returned nothing, so it was done by hand.

### The find that reframed it

**Vine position is a grid ordinal, not a sequence index.** One pergola
row holds 47 vines across positions 5 to 59 with eight numbers missing.
Every vacant space inside a planted run has been in the data since the
vine import, and nothing had ever read it.

A new view makes it queryable, counting only gaps *inside* each row's
occupied range — a row that starts late may be short rather than missing
vines at the end, and assuming otherwise would invent spaces. One site
carries meaningful vacancy; the other is negligible. The count it
produces for the pergola independently corroborates the spaces/planted
split derived earlier from a completely different source.

### Findings

**The headline yield figure answers a grid question with a planted
count.** Effective area is computed as picked vines divided by density,
and every tonnes-per-hectare divides by it. Vacant spaces in a picked
extent are ground the pick walked over and did not count, so the area is
understated and the yield **overstated by the vacancy rate of whatever
was picked** — several percent at one site, negligible at the other.

**Extent arithmetic pro-rates a planted count across a row range.** "How
many vines does this range cover" is a grid question. Now that vine
position is known to be a grid ordinal, the better fix is not to swap in
spaces but to **stop pro-rating at all** — count the actual vine rows in
the range, which is exact rather than estimated.

**The new spaces column is wrong everywhere except the one planting a
source distinguished.** It was seeded from the planted count, so every
other planting's "spaces" is understated by its vacancy. Now derivable
from the position gaps.

**Two layers label the same field identically and mean different
things** — one counts spaces, the other counts planted vines, differing
by several hundred estate-wide. Nothing currently mixes them in one
calculation.

### Checked and clean

Clone and rootstock shares correctly use planted vines — a vacant space
belongs to no clone. A drawn shape's vine count is correctly planted.
Computed vine positions are correct, which I had expected to be a defect
and it is not. The landing totals are internally consistent within their
own layer. Kilograms per vine is correct.

One worth knowing rather than fixing: the spacing check measures what the
position computation placed, so it validates the interpolation rather
than reality — it cannot detect a mis-stated spacing that both sides
share.

Reported, not fixed, as asked.

## 4. Working practice

Two rules added to the project's own instructions file:

**Establish the baseline before blaming the change.** Before attributing
an error to the thing you just changed, reproduce it with that thing
untouched. The stale-cache episode is written up as the worked example,
including the point that a wrong attribution left uncorrected is worse
than the bug, because the next session builds on it.

**End every session with a copyable handoff** — one block, plain text,
covering what changed, what was verified and how, what does not fit, what
was left undone and any open defects, keeping the candour about
uncertainty.
