## Evening: the season model, from first principles
- Mike asked for a first-principles look at the model under weather, growth-stage forecasting, disease pressure and spray planning, noting that the Gubler-Thomas powdery mildew index reads 100 for much of a Central Otago season and so is not useful. Run as a workflow of 21 agents: readers over the code, six researchers across the literature, four independent designs, three judges, a synthesis and three critics.
- The design (`docs/season-model.md`): the index is an interval clock, not a measure of pressure, kept only as published beside its own climatology; the model keeps cover, host susceptibility, inoculum (a recorded state, never a weather event) and a weather rate with brakes apart per block per day, with the word from a printed rule table; stage gates outrank weather; stages are a reading first; the weather is fixed, scored by lead time against the vineyard's station from the forecast archive, then corrected per site; every daily figure is kept and scored at vintage end. The critics cut the first season to what one person can record on the existing walk and stopped the clock from setting spray intervals until its bands are local.
- Forecast archives: lead-time archives begin in 2024, the ensemble is archived nowhere (so it is stored from day one), and the archives sit on a paid tier whose licence covers a business; a decade-deep archive exists for the coarser GFS model.

- Week one of the weather work: four defects in the weather cascade were found by reading and reproduced in tests before being fixed. A logger's day had been reduced to one reading from midday the next day (UTC date keys); a season's own-station readings stopped at the API's 1,000-row page; the buddy check compared a logger with the model's daily mean and flagged warm afternoons; the spray advice filtered a status that does not exist. The day is now built in the database by the New Zealand calendar, with the same rule in TypeScript and a live test comparing the two across the 23- and 25-hour clock-change days; the buddy is a logger only; the scheduled pull reaches back a day in bounded steps.
