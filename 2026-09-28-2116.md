# 2026-09-28 21:16 — The ferment view: picks to barrels, and today's reading in a tap

*Block layouts, vine counts, yields, input products and rates, client
names and certification details are withheld from this public copy.
Here that means every kg and litre figure, the litres-per-kilo factors
(yields), the addition rates, and the blend and lot names, which can
name a client's label. The private copy has them.*

Mike asked for the ferment view — the bridge between harvest and
barrelling down — designed against last vintage's ferments already in
the database, with "don't build until I've seen" what we hold. Then
built, applied to live and released on his yes.

## What last vintage held

The picks, the intake chemistry, the pressing and the barrels had been
imported; **the daily ferment readings had not.** Both weigh-sheet
workbooks carry a typed daily log — one row per tank per day, a month of
Brix, pH, two temperatures, the cap work and a comment for every tank.
Each fermenter's own sheet turned out to be a spreadsheet filter over
that log, and not a safe one to read: one sheet's labels sit a column
away from its values, and the two temperature labels are swapped
between the log and the fermenter sheets.

## Mike's answers

Cap work is plunge down, pump over and **jug over**. There are two
litres-per-kilo figures with two purposes: an initial estimate of the
liquid in the tank, used for additions, and a lower, realistic one for
what reaches barrel. **Day 1** is the day the first pick goes into the
tank (days on skins), and a kg-weighted average harvest date is quoted.
The fermenter sheets' temperature labels are right — I had recommended
the log's. And, more important than any of the data: **an intuitive app
where a simple input or search needs no wading through detail**; he
verifies imported data against his spreadsheets himself.

## What was built

- Two event types on the lot, in the cellar book: a **ferment reading**
  and an **addition**. A vineyard hand or read-only login is refused by
  row-level security; a phone's retry lands once.
- Two summary views: each lot's first pick and weighted harvest date,
  and its latest recorded values and press date — so no list meets the
  API's 1,000-row limit.
- The daily log imported from the typed sheets only: tanks mapped to
  lots through the pressing records, per vineyard (tank codes repeat
  across the two sites); comments word for word; a stray backtick before
  a number read as the number; a day with two plunges kept as two; a
  "?" kept as written, not counted; a guard that refuses the import if
  any reading lacks its lot or vessel.
- A **Ferments** tab. The list finds a tank or lot by typing; a pressed
  ferment is one line; an active one shows its latest numbers. A
  ferment opens on the tank, a status line, the latest numbers, today's
  reading (the tank, asked once; a couple of numbers; a tap for the cap
  work; held on the phone with no signal) and the chart — Brix and both
  temperatures by day on skins, cap work, additions and notes marked,
  other fermenters laid over. Where it came from, where it went, and
  every reading are one-line summaries that open.

## Verified

Unit tests, including two real tank-days proving the temperature
mapping. On a restored copy of live: the import's counts, the views,
the whole flow in a browser, the permission rules by role, a repeated
send refused as already saved, the full suite. Then live: applied, every
test passes against live, typecheck and build clean, released, and the
live-site and sign-in checks pass.

## Found

- The imported intake "measured" volumes are all the sheet's own
  kg-times-factor formula, not measurements; the view no longer calls
  them measured.
- A third of the tanks' press dates differ between the sheet and the
  ledger by a few days; left for Mike, since the ledger is append-only.
- The chart's axis could start inside the data, which would have drawn
  a dry ferment's negative Brix off the chart; fixed and tested.

## Where I was wrong

The temperature labels (above); a column name I had already got wrong
that morning, caught on the local copy before live; and a first version
that put everything on one screen.
