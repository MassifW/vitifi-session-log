# 21 Oct 2026: compliance forms (checklists, records and signed documents on a phone)

- Request: the checklists and forms that the wine industry's audit schemes and health and safety law ask for beyond tracing (health and safety, training records, contractor agreements, visitor records, pre-harvest checklists, maintenance logs), read, ticked, signed and dated on a phone or tablet, plus a master list of what is needed and an improvement of the paper and spreadsheet versions already in use. Which schemes apply, and what the winery's own documents say, stays in the private log.
- Built on a branch, database change not applied: a Compliance tab with 37 forms written as data (no winery's name in any of them), a library of the winery's own policies to read and sign, a signature pad that takes a finger, and a "needs attention" list (overdue checks, lapsing certificates, documents waiting for a signature, unfinished drafts).
- Design: one table holds every filled-in form, together with a copy of the form as it was that day, so a signed record always shows what was signed even after the form changes. A read-and-sign record copies the document text that was read. The database stamps who and when, and refuses to complete a record until the signatures its form requires are on it. A signature is a drawn picture plus a printed name and the server's time: a record that a named person signed on a device, not a legal electronic-signature scheme. Some records (injuries, employment) are private to their author and the owners.
- Verification: the full test suite passes (1705, with the live-database suites skipped in a cloud session), typecheck and build pass, and the form was driven in a phone-size browser. The SQL's own checks (31 of them, as throwaway staff through row-level security) have not been run: there was no database to run them on.
- Found while testing: a scripted signing failed on one of two pads because the page's sticky button bar covered the box; and the document to read was drawn below the "I have read this" boxes, so it now sits under the picker.
- Not built: signing in from a QR code on the visitor's own phone (needs a public address that writes to the database, so a security decision first), filling in without signal, photos inside a form, and an audit screen over the records.
