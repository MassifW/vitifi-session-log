# 2 Nov 2026: sharing with a client's own winery, both ways (step 6 of many wineries)

*Public copy. Client names are withheld; nothing else of substance is left out.*

**Asked:** build the client sharing already designed: a contract client with its own vineyard-only winery in VITIFI can be shown what the winery that makes its wine holds about its fruit and wine, and can share its spray and pick records back. The owner's three answers stood: the client must accept; shares by kind per client with a hide on single lots; both directions built together. On a branch, the database change NOT on live, tried on a restored copy, a pull request for the second developer's review.

**Built (one migration, not on live):**
- Three tables (a link between two wineries, the kinds each side shares, and a lot or vineyard kept back). Each row carries the winery that owns it, behind the same row-level wall as every table, with no grant to any login.
- Functions, none callable with the public key: invite, accept, end, switch a kind, keep something back (owners only, each checking role and side), a list of one's links, and named read functions: lots, picks, ferment readings, analyses and bottled stock (winery to client); sprays with each product's withholding periods, and picks (client to winery). Every read needs an accepted link and the other side's kind switched on, returns named fields only (never notes, rates, whole records or who recorded what), and the winery's kinds cover only lots whose fruit is all that client's, less any kept back.
- A test function that makes three wineries (the winery, the client's own, and a stranger with a client of the same name) and checks 34 things as owners, a vineyard hand and a read-only colleague, then rolls back.
- Screens: "Share with this client" under each client; "A winery that makes your wine" on People (check a code, then accept); a Shared tab shown only to a winery with a link.

**Choices made where the design was silent, for the owner to confirm before live:** a 12-letter invitation code made by the winery and typed by the client's owner (an email address would let anyone ask whether an address uses VITIFI); everyone who can read at the receiving winery sees what is shared, only owners change it; the field lists; the client's hide is by vineyard; either owner can end a link. The brief said to ask before such choices; I built on them so there is something to look at, and said so.

**Verified on a local restored copy:**
- The test function 34 of 34, and it names the breakage when its rules are removed on purpose (3, 5 and 2 failures for three different removals).
- The existing isolation checks between wineries stayed clean; the security-surface test lists and justifies the 15 new owner-run functions.
- The whole suite against the copy: 2,653 of 2,654 on the first run, the one failure mine (the readable-export test caught the new tables in no file and not excluded); fixed, then 2,654 of 2,654 with a second winery present. Typecheck and build pass.
- In a browser, two logins side by side (a test owner of the winery, and a vineyard-only client winery made by the new-winery script): invite, accept (code typed in lower case with spaces), the Shared tab appearing at once, each side switching kinds on and keeping a vineyard and a lot back, each seeing only what was shared, ending the link leaving nothing in the database. No console errors; no sideways scroll at phone width.
- Two fixes from the browser: ferment readings with no figures showed as empty rows (now left out), and ending a link used the delete control whose button says "Delete for good" although no record goes (sharing now has its own two-step "End the link").

**Not tried:** the nightly restore drill with the new tables; analyses and stock in the browser (no such records for the client on the copy; the test function covers both).

**Then:** the owner confirmed the first choice, the invitation code. The other four still wait for him.
