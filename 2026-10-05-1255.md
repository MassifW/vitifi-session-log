## The yield forecast

**Read:**
- **The owner's spreadsheets.** One season's in full, every formula, and
  this year's two, the shape in use now. Other years exist and were not
  opened yet.
- **Skinkis & McLaughlin (2022), Catalyst 6:30-37**, on Oregon Pinot
  noir:
  - the lag phase falls 49-61 days after half the flower caps are off;
  - the lag-to-harvest cluster weight factor averages 2.1;
  - seed hardness is not a good guide;
  - they advise each grower to build their own history.
  - Their curve for the factor by days since flowering is coarse (an
    error of about 0.6). By my arithmetic it gives about 1.65 at day 55,
    against the 2.1 they measured.
- **WSU Extension EM086E** (Komm & Moyer, 2015):
  - most of the error in estimates comes from bunch counts, not weights;
  - how many clusters to weigh;
  - lag-phase estimates are less accurate than near-harvest ones.
- **An AWRI technical note**, after Clingeleffer et al. (2001):
  - bunches per vine explain about 60% of season-to-season variation,
    berries per bunch 30%, berry weight 10%;
  - estimates are about ±20% from spring counts, ±10-15% after fruit set
    and ±5% near harvest.
- **Bragato Research Institute's Grape Yield Analyser programme:**
  cameras and radar counting bunches. Relevant to the "AI later" wish.

**The method, as the spreadsheets work it:**
- bunches per vine from bunches counted bay by bay;
- times a harvest bunch weight, estimated from bunches weighed:
  - at the lag phase: × 2.2, with 1.8 and 2.6 as its range;
  - at first berry colour: × 1.8, kept within that range;
  - just before harvest: as weighed;
- times the productive vines, less a loss allowance;
- then litres, at the winery's litres per kg, and barrels.
- **How sure** is each sample's 95% interval as a share of its mean, and
  the sample size that would give ±10%.

**Found:** in one past season, every block's first-colour estimate came
out above the lag range's ceiling, so every estimate sat at the
ceiling. The harvest came in above the forecast. One season can't say
which factor is off; the other years' sheets could.

**Built:** a small module that does this arithmetic, with the factors and
settings always passed in as the vineyard's own data. Its tests feed it
that season's own inputs and get the spreadsheet's own results back, to
the spreadsheet's decimals.

**Proposed, waiting for the owner's answers:**
1. Bunch counts and bunch weights recorded against a parcel, like every
   other reading: append-only, and held on the phone with no signal.
2. A forecast per parcel, vineyard and destination wine. It names what
   is missing rather than showing a zero, and during vintage shows what
   is still to come.
3. The factors as settings.
4. Each parcel's likely lag-phase window, worked out from its own
   flowering reading.
5. After harvest, forecast against picked, so the vineyard's own factors
   replace the starting ones in time.
6. Later, the past years' spreadsheets brought in.
- **Questions for the owner:**
  - where it lives in the app;
  - how productive vines are counted (the app's vine map doesn't record
    them);
  - whether a pre-harvest weight is used as weighed;
  - whether to import the history.
