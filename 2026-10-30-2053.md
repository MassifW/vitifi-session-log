# 30 Oct 2026 — The accounting link: registered, and built

- **The owner asked** what it would take to see dollar values beside each wine's bottles on "Where it went". The answer is a read-only link to the winery's accounting system (Xero).
- **The owner registered the developer app**, walked through one step at a time:
  - a web app with two return addresses, the live site's and the laptop's;
  - "train an AI model": no;
  - Xero's minimum security requirements: yes. I read them first.
- **Xero's security standard.** It asks for:
  - tokens only on the server;
  - the long-lived token encrypted at rest, under a key kept outside the database;
  - TLS;
  - a return address that only redirects;
  - breaches reported.

  That meant a third secret, the encryption key. The owner made it with a command that put it straight on the clipboard, so it never appeared on screen. All three values went into the hosting settings and the local env file by hand. I checked the local file's form without printing any value.
- **Built on a branch, nothing on live yet:**
  - three tables, all readable only by owners, enforced on the row: the connection with its sealed token, the invoice lines as read, and the words an owner says name a wine on invoices that carry no item code;
  - two routes for connecting;
  - actions to bring in invoices (only those changed since last time) and to disconnect;
  - the money on "Where it went": excluding GST, converted at each invoice's own rate, approved or paid invoices only. It shows each price a bottle went at, customers and years, and how many sold bottles have no invoice price.
  - No use of the service key was added: everything runs as the signed-in owner.
- **Tests:** 20 new tests cover the arithmetic, the sealing, and the rules that keep tokens off the browser.
- **Tried for real the same evening.** The migration went onto the live database first. Then, from the laptop, the owner connected the winery's organisation and VITIFI read every sales invoice.
  - **The rate runs the right way:** dividing by the invoice's rate gave the figure worked by hand; multiplying would have been well short.
  - **One wine's total matched exactly:** bottles, money and average all agreed with the figures worked by hand that morning.
- **Wrong at first, and fixed:**
  - The list of invoice lines with no item code showed every service and freight line. Now only lines that look like one of the winery's wines are listed, with the likely wine picked for the owner to confirm.
  - A "bottles with no price" figure was really a net of two opposite gaps, and now says so.
  - A name given to the wrong wine can now be taken back.
- **Still unchecked:** the address the hosting reports, which will show on the first live connection.
- **Not yet read:** credit notes.
- **Then the money by reason.** The owner asked for the dollars and average price on each reason a wine left (export, trade, the broker and so on). An invoice says who bought, and a sign-out says why, so each customer is given one kind of sale, once, with the likely kind suggested. Where one invoice mixes two kinds, all of it counts as the customer's kind, a known and accepted error.
- **Released the same night,** after the owner's review. Both migrations were on the live database first, the checks passed on the released commit itself, and the three after-release checks passed, including the accounting keys being absent from the site's code. Reading changed invoices on the live site worked, which proved the live server's key unseals what the laptop sealed.
