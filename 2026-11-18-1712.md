# A soil-family map from oblique drone photos

18 November 2026. Mike pointed at a folder of drone photos from one December morning that match how he reads the vineyard's
two soil families by eye (drier, yellowish ground against greener ground), and asked for a map from them, checked against
what we already hold.

**Built.** A picture and three map layers (the photos flattened onto the map, the greenness of the ground, a two-class
split). Not uploaded yet. No code changed, nothing written to the live database.

**How.** The photos are oblique, taken from 70 to 120 m up, and each carries the drone's position and camera angle. Each
block was flattened from one photo by a camera model built from those, then corrected block by block from outline corners
marked by eye. Fit about 3 to 5 m. Greenness is green-against-red averaged over a few rows, split at one value for the whole
vineyard so no block is forced to hold both classes.

**Found.**
- Most of what Mike had said from the ground, none of it used to draw the map, agrees with it; one statement is unclear and
  one disagrees. The per-block detail is in the private log (block layouts are withheld here).
- Notes made with an auger when two blocks were planted separate cleanly on the new map (clay and loam on green ground,
  gravel and rock on dry), which an older conductivity survey never managed.
- A drone survey from a different season repeats the pattern in half the blocks and not in the others.
- A DJI photo's recorded *gimbal* heading was about 25 degrees off true here; the *flight* heading was right.

**Where I was wrong or wasteful.**
- I copied very large survey rasters into a temporary folder and filled the computer's disk; I removed my copies and now
  read them in place.
- Three automatic ways of fitting the photos failed before I marked corners by eye, which is why the fit is metres, not
  better.
- I first took the 25-degree heading error for magnetic declination.

**Not done.** Layers not uploaded; most blocks seen from one direction only; one morning in one season is not a soil survey,
and green ground has causes other than soil (irrigation, mowing, what was sown).

## Later the same session: watering, and a mistake of mine

Mike added what the photos cannot know: some blocks hold young vines that are watered more and so look greener than their
soil, and one block had gone unwatered for two seasons and so looks yellower.

- **My mistake.** The one statement I reported as disagreeing with the map did not disagree. I had measured "the eastern
  rows" of a block by east-west position; its rows run diagonally, so that was one end of the block, not its eastern rows.
  Measured across the rows, the map says what Mike said.
- **Re-cut.** The blocks whose watering differs are now split at their own value instead of the vineyard-wide one. For
  those, the map says where a block is greener or drier within itself, not how it compares with its neighbours.
- One corner of one block is in no usable photo and stays blank.

## Later again

Mike named one more block whose watering makes it look drier than its soil. It is now split at its own value too. How far
that moves it is a choice of method, not a measurement, and is his to confirm by eye.

## Later again: the split set to Mike's reading

Mike judged the whole map about a tenth too dry, mostly in three blocks. The cut-offs were moved to match, with the rows he
had said were genuinely lean held where they were. The split is now his reading laid over the photos' ranking of the ground
within each block, so it is no longer an independent check of his eye. My first redraw wrongly turned those lean rows green;
corrected.

## Later again: one unphotographed corner

One corner of one block is in no photo that shows the ground (the only views are distant and grazing, where the rows read as
solid canopy). In the split it is now filled from the nearest measured ground and drawn paler as an estimate, for Mike to
confirm from the ground. The greenness layer still leaves it blank.

## Later again

Mike confirmed the estimated corner and asked for the boundary there to sit a little further one way; done by hand, over the
photo's reading where they differ, and recorded as his. It took me four tries to move only what he asked for.

## Later again: two more nudges, and label colours

Two more areas were moved a little towards one soil family on Mike's word, and the split is now drawn in the two wines'
label colours, sampled from bottle photographs. That reverses the look of earlier copies of the picture, which the write-up
now warns about.

## Later again: colours by soil

The label colours did not read well: the leaner soil's label is the darker, heavier-looking colour. The split is now drawn
by soil instead, a clay brown for the heavier family and a schist grey for the stony one. The upload to the map is not done:
the built-in browser was not signed in and cannot attach a file to the upload form.

## Later again: on the live map

The three layers are on the live map, added through the app's own upload form in Mike's signed-in browser, on his say-so.
Found while doing it: the form resets when the previous layer finishes cutting its tiles, silently dropping a file chosen in
the meantime; choosing it again works.

## Later again: a layer that was never drawn

One of the three layers I had reported as uploaded had never finished cutting its tiles, because I closed the browser tab
too soon; it drew nothing, and I had not looked. Found when Mike asked about the map. Uploaded again and watched it finish.
The dead row is still listed: the app has not marked it as stopped after two and a half hours, so the screen offers no way to
remove or redo it. That is a fault to look into.

## 20 November: the dead layer row cleared

The row that would not clear: a tiling job's "last heard from" time was the row's last-changed time, and any saved change to
the row (showing, fading, reordering) moves that, so a change two and a half hours after the job died made it look alive for
ten more minutes. Two days on it read as stopped. A stopped layer offers "tile it again" but no way to remove it, so its tiles
were finished first and then it was taken off the map. Both faults are noted and not yet fixed.

## 20 November: the two Layers panel faults fixed, on a branch

A layer whose tiling has stopped can now be taken off the map directly. And a tiling job's "last heard from" time is now a
column of its own that only the tiler moves, so hiding or fading a layer no longer makes a dead job look alive. The database
change is written and tested but not yet applied to the live system.

## 20 November: released

The Layers panel fix is live. The full test run before release caught a new database check function of mine running with
more rights than it needed; a one-line follow-up made it run as its caller, and the release went out after that.

## 20 November: a middle class between the two soil families, and a post-painting map

A third class, Transition, now sits along the boundaries between the two soil families, at least two rows wide, drawn from
the drone photographs with an old conductivity survey, a later drone survey and planting-day auger notes used only where each
held up block by block. The owner's own statements about particular ground are hard rules. From it, a sheet per block marking
every trellis post by class, for painting the post tops, with posts to check on the ground ringed: where the records seem to
lack a post, and a corner no photograph saw. Independent checks found something in each of four rounds; all were fixed.

## 20 November, afternoon: a trough drawn from the owner's description

The owner described a trough running across one block between two ridges, on the line of a gap between two other blocks,
deeper at one end. It was drawn to follow the greenest ground near that line, refined four times on his word, and checked
independently each time; each check found something in my drawing (a notch, the trough off the green ground, a bug that
pinned the search at its limit, ruler-straight edges), all fixed before it replaced the layers on the live map. The public
website map's second round applied the owner's publishing decisions and now reads vine status from the nightly backup.
- The public website map is on a private deploy preview from a draft pull request (not merged). On the preview the map
  loads under the site's real security headers with nothing blocked.
- The website map now carries each vine's clone, rootstock and planting year from the vineyard records, with a "colour by"
  choice like the app's own vine view, on the private preview only. Fields the records leave mostly blank, and supplier
  and irrigation details, are not published.
- The owner set a planting year for older vines with none recorded (those not marked young). The public map applies it
  when the file is made, not in the records, and says on the map that the year is presumed rather than recorded; young
  vines with no year stay "not recorded".

## 21 November, morning: the presumed planting year written into the records

At the owner's word, the presumed planting year was written into the vineyard records for the older vines with no year
recorded and not marked young. It was checked read-only first (the count matched what the map showed, the disk had ample
room, no backup was running), written in one statement that would have refused any other count, and checked afterwards
block by block. Young vines with no year, and the other vineyard, were left alone. The previous night's backup holds the
records as they were.
- The owner gave the remaining young vines with no recorded year their planting year; it was written into the records
  the same careful way (count checked before and after, one statement that refused any other count).
- The public map's block outlines now hold every vine: widened only where vines sat past the recorded line, rounded
  along the row ends. The app's own outlines are unchanged, because their area feeds per-hectare work; changing them is
  the owner's decision. An independent check found several faults in the first version (row-by-row teeth, dots cut by
  the line, a soil-fill ordering bug, a stale test); all fixed. One block got its proper name on the website.
- The colour views now draw each vine as a borderless tile, so the colours fill the rows instead of sitting in ringed
  dots.
- A second independent check found three more faults, all fixed: a lone vine's tile turned the wrong way, tiles crossing
  block lines, and two neighbouring blocks' widened outlines overlapping by a thin sliver. A test now fails if any two
  outlines overlap or cross. Fixing the overlap took several tries; the earlier claim that none overlapped was wrong.
- One block's planting was renamed in the records at the owner's word. The website map's colour views no longer draw
  block outlines (the colours show the blocks); the outline returns on hover and for keyboard focus.
- The map's row-stepping buttons now carry arrows showing which way each neighbouring row lies.
- The website's illustrated vineyard map had soil marks from an earlier, partial reading of the same drone photos; it
  agreed where it marked ground but covered about a quarter of it. Its soil marks were redrawn from the new soil map
  across every block, then checked independently; the check also found the map's credit understated the sources the
  soil map draws on, and the owner is asked which sources may stand behind a public map.
- The second vineyard has no soil map yet; the owner will draw one to start from.
