# A soil-family map from oblique drone photos

18 November 2026. Mike pointed at a folder of drone photos from one December morning that match how he reads the vineyard's
two soil families by eye (drier, yellowish ground against greener ground), and asked for a map from them, checked against
what we already hold.

**Built.** A picture and three map layers (the photos flattened onto the map, the greenness of the ground, a two-class
split). Not uploaded yet. No code changed, nothing written to the live database.

**How.** The photos are oblique, taken from 70 to 120 m up, and each carries the drone's position and camera angle. Each
block was flattened from one photo by a camera model built from those, then corrected block by block from outline corners
marked by eye. Fit about 3 to 5 m. Greenness is green-against-red averaged over a few rows, split at one value for the whole
vineyard so no block is forced to hold both classes.

**Found.**
- Most of what Mike had said from the ground, none of it used to draw the map, agrees with it; one statement is unclear and
  one disagrees. The per-block detail is in the private log (block layouts are withheld here).
- Notes made with an auger when two blocks were planted separate cleanly on the new map (clay and loam on green ground,
  gravel and rock on dry), which an older conductivity survey never managed.
- A drone survey from a different season repeats the pattern in half the blocks and not in the others.
- A DJI photo's recorded *gimbal* heading was about 25 degrees off true here; the *flight* heading was right.

**Where I was wrong or wasteful.**
- I copied very large survey rasters into a temporary folder and filled the computer's disk; I removed my copies and now
  read them in place.
- Three automatic ways of fitting the photos failed before I marked corners by eye, which is why the fit is metres, not
  better.
- I first took the 25-degree heading error for magnetic declination.

**Not done.** Layers not uploaded; most blocks seen from one direction only; one morning in one season is not a soil survey,
and green ground has causes other than soil (irrigation, mowing, what was sown).

## Later the same session: watering, and a mistake of mine

Mike added what the photos cannot know: some blocks hold young vines that are watered more and so look greener than their
soil, and one block had gone unwatered for two seasons and so looks yellower.

- **My mistake.** The one statement I reported as disagreeing with the map did not disagree. I had measured "the eastern
  rows" of a block by east-west position; its rows run diagonally, so that was one end of the block, not its eastern rows.
  Measured across the rows, the map says what Mike said.
- **Re-cut.** The blocks whose watering differs are now split at their own value instead of the vineyard-wide one. For
  those, the map says where a block is greener or drier within itself, not how it compares with its neighbours.
- One corner of one block is in no usable photo and stays blank.

## Later again

Mike named one more block whose watering makes it look drier than its soil. It is now split at its own value too. How far
that moves it is a choice of method, not a measurement, and is his to confirm by eye.
