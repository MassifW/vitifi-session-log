# 2026-09-19 22:50 — Plantings, a prior vintage imported, and a correction by adjustment

*Yields, volumes, vine counts and composition percentages are withheld
from this public copy. The findings, the bugs and the reasoning are all
here; only the production figures are held back.*

Three fixes, then importing an earlier vintage.

## 1. A missing parcel: neither topping loss nor a lost barrel

Tested rather than assumed, and the answer turned out to be a third
thing neither hypothesis covered.

The barrel log keeps its own per-lot totals row and repeats it once per
tasting round. The three oldest rounds read one figure and the ten newer
ones read a slightly smaller one, the difference being exactly one
nominal barrel. Two barrels also vanish from the log partway through,
which made "an unrecorded barrel" look plausible.

Every totals row is a live `SUM` over its own round's barrel rows, and
every barrel row's volume is a hardcoded literal — so each round is an
independent reading, not one number echoed thirteen times. Summing each
round's barrel rows gives **the same figure in all thirteen**, across
sixteen months. Nothing declines anywhere. So it is not topping loss:
that would show as falling barrel volumes. And the two barrels that do
leave the log are different lots and account for neither.

What it actually is: a lot named **TopBlend**, assembled from seven
other lots, recorded in the three oldest rounds **with no barrel code
against it**, and dropped from the log entirely thereafter. The older
totals include it because their SUM range covers its row; the newer ones
do not because it is gone.

So the larger figure is the volume at barrel-down including a topping
blend that never had a vessel, and the smaller is what sat in barrels.
Its disappearance is consistent with being used to top the other
barrels — which is what a topping blend is for — but the log records no
consumption, transfer or loss, so that stays the likely explanation
rather than a recorded fact.

## 2. Clone and rootstock now derive through plantings

New `plantings` (block, row range, spacing, area) and
`planting_composition` (clone, rootstock, vine count), imported from each
vineyard's own vine-info sheet. One site reconciles exactly to its
sheet's own totals row.

The chain is barrel → lot → pick → planting → clone/rootstock, and the
planting is the key, not the block. One block alone is four plantings on
three different rootstocks, which is why the previous pass returned
clone and rootstock as unavailable rather than guessing from block.

**The derived figures disagree with the barrel log's own columns, and the
derivation is the better of the two.** The log maps one clone per block;
the planting data shows one block is roughly 60/40 between two clones,
so the log understates one and overstates the other. The derivation also
surfaces an interplanted rootstock that the log's fixed set of columns
has nowhere to put. Derived block percentages agree with the log to
within about one percentage point, which is the check that the chain is
sound.

Found and fixed a real bug doing this: provenance was stored as one
value per lot and each pick overwrote the last, so a lot picked from two
blocks kept only one of them. **An entire block had disappeared from
every blend it was in.** Every dimension is now a weight-weighted
distribution across all of a lot's picks.

The second site's sheet needed care — its Chardonnay rows shift columns,
and twelve composition lines were dropped and reported rather than
imported, including a large one whose "rootstock" is not a rootstock at
all. Its bay-level breakdown is a finer cut of the same vines and was
left out to avoid double-counting the site.

## 3. Two more destination blends

The second site's own blend names were missing from the controlled
vocabulary, so its barrels read as undecided when they were in fact
assigned. Added, and most of them now resolve. Done as an update, not a
superseding event: the events already named the blends in their notes,
and writing a revision would have put a decision in each barrel's
history that the winemaker never made. The genuine slash-combinations
stay undecided because they are, and one unrecognised code is left
unresolved rather than guessed at.

## Importing the earlier vintage

**As recorded, uncorrected.** The misbooking found last session — one
lot's volume entered against another — is imported exactly as the log
has it, so the reconciliation has the real discrepancy to find. Live
from the database, the over-lot flags as impossible, the under-lot as
missing volume, and `matched_pairs` proposes the specific barrels to
move, with the basis *"the log already records these barrels as \<the
under-lot\>"*.

**The correction as a dated adjustment.** New movements of type
`adjustment`, dated, attributed to a named person, each pointing at the
movement it replaces through `supersedes_movement_id`. The originals are
neither edited nor deleted, so both states are recoverable — the log as
written, and the log as corrected. A new `movement_current` view takes
the head of each chain so the superseded booking is not double-counted.

**No standing exclusion for the non-Pinot lot.** The vintage counts every
kilogram picked. Recovery is scoped by variety instead, via a new
`vintage_variety_balance` view, which reproduces the winery's own
working figure without a rule anyone has to remember — and which keeps
working when there is more than one exception.

**Lots gained a vintage and a variety.** The lot name was globally
unique, which does not survive a second vintage: nine names are used by
both vintages for different wine. Uniqueness is now (name, vintage).

### Two bugs the second vintage exposed

Both were invisible with only one vintage loaded and would have stayed
invisible.

1. **Attribution followed the movement's own lot rather than its lines.**
   For the misbooked barrels the movement names one lot and the line
   names another. Attributing by the movement credited the wrong one —
   exactly backwards — and made the discrepancy vanish entirely: nothing
   flagged. The line is the authoritative statement of whose wine it is;
   that is what a header/line ledger is for.
2. **The matched-pairs check searched barrels by the same wrong key**, so
   it proposed moving the over-lot's own barrels rather than the
   misbooked ones. It now finds barrels through the lines, sorts
   misbooked ones first (the line credits one lot while the movement is
   filed under another — that is the signature), and prefers a pair the
   source log already points at over one that merely balances
   arithmetically. Without that preference, several zero-recovery lots
   scored identically and it paired with an alias artefact instead of the
   right lot.

### What did not come across

- **Two parcels with no vessel.** A movement has to land in a vessel and
  neither has one, so the imported in-barrel volume is short of the
  log's barrel-down figure by exactly those two.
- **Block, clone and rootstock composition for the earlier vintage.** Its
  daily ferment log records tank and weight but not which rows were
  picked, so those picks carry no area and the planting chain has nothing
  to attach to. Deliberately not inferred from lot names — the names
  encode block and clone loosely enough that inference would be wrong
  more often than right, as two specific lots demonstrate.
- **Most barrel-down dates.** Only a handful of ferments record a press
  date. The rest fall back to the harvest date and every movement's notes
  say which basis it used.

account needed. It has its own root commit and shares no history with the
app's own repository, so nothing can leak through a common ancestor.

Every file in every commit was scanned for this session's production
