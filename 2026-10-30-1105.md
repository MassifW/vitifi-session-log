# 30 Oct 2026, sensor research Phase 1: the market survey

- The aim (Mike and Tucker, Ampel): software that takes in data from any sensor or source is more useful and more attractive to other growers; and there is interest in building hardware, starting from Tucker's spore-trap work. Four phases with a stop after each: survey the market; what is worth it for us; what we could build; a plan for taking in any source, with no code until it is approved.
- Phase 1 written: `research/01-market-survey.md` (tables by category: weather and frost, public weather data, soil, plant water status and imaging, fruit, spore traps and disease models, pests, water and spray telematics, connectivity, and who lets you read your own data), with NZ$ prices at late-October rates, NZ supply, and independent evidence kept apart from makers' claims. The five category notes behind it are in `research/01-notes/` with their sources.
- Method and its limits: five research agents in parallel shared one search allowance, which ran out partway; after that only known pages could be read, and many shops and journals refused automated reads. Gaps are marked rather than filled. The two most load-bearing pages were re-read by hand.
- Findings: a 2025-26 NZ spore-trap pilot that included Central Otago started sprays on the Gubler-Thomas model, skipped two early rounds with no difference in disease, and traps caught nothing until model risk had built; the samples went to US labs, and no NZ or Australian lab sells a powdery mildew DNA test. No automated spore trap is independently validated for grape powdery mildew. Several NZ services keep their data APIs for a custom-priced top tier. An independent 2026 study of all-in-one weather stations found temperature robust and rain the weakest reading on every unit. For powdery mildew, canopy temperature matters more than leaf wetness. No public LoRaWAN coverage near the sites; NZ 3G is off; satellite IoT through One NZ is live. MetService's station API is in beta; NIWA's CliFlo is retired.
- Corrected before commit: three overstatements in my own draft (a model's validation described as all-NZ, a citation pointing at the wrong source, an API described as open when it is in beta), and one fact gone stale the same morning (Pisa now reads a neighbour's station).
- Found in passing: a schema test misreads a nested object in one database call as extra arguments, a false positive on main; offered as its own fix.
- Gates: tests (one failure, the false positive above), typecheck, build. Phases 2 to 4 wait on seven questions to Mike and Tucker.
