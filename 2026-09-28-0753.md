and let each line say where its fruit came from at whatever level is

## The decisions

Mike's answers to the harvest questions: conditions cover both the

plan was approved, with a block added as a level; bins have a standard

## The test bed

Docker Desktop, its signature checked before running, installed through
the native macOS password prompt. The repository's migrations cannot be
replayed into a fresh database — some name IDs only the live database
generated — so the test database was **the latest nightly backup,
restored into a local Supabase**, as the nightly check does. Deleted
afterwards.

That restore showed two gaps in the backup check, raised as a task:
Supabase's role dump drops a role grant the test functions need, and the
comparison hashes a computed area column that an ARM Mac rounds
differently from live's x86 servers in the last digits.

## What was built

- A harvest pick may now point at no area or lot, **provided it has a
  line** — checked at commit by a constraint trigger, because a check
  constraint cannot look at another table.
- Each line names a vineyard, block, parcel, rows, a drawn area or exact
  vines (vine-map positions); only fruit weight and one level are
  required. Bins carry their empty weight as weighed.
- **`record_harvest_pick()` saves the whole pick in one transaction**,
  as whoever is signed in. It replaced four separate requests, where a
  failure after the first left a pick with no lines that the retry then
  reported as "already saved".
- A provenance view works out what each line comes to — vines, clone,
  rootstock — and marks it estimated where apportioned; the phone applies
  the same rules offline.
- Weather fills itself in: a station's readings during the picking hours
  where one records through the day, otherwise the day's figures,
  labelled; what was saved says whether it was filled in, typed over, or
  typed. The fruit's condition is a list to tap.
- A live-database test records a pick at every level as a throwaway
  vineyard hand and rolls it all back — nothing it writes survives.

## Found by testing

1. The rebuilt pick-detail view re-found a drawn area's vines against
   every vine in the estate on every read — over a second each time. It
   now counts the vines resolved when the pick was saved.
2. The live test function took over 8 seconds and **timed out through
   the API**. Measured: as a signed-in person every vine row passes the
   permission check separately — about 25 times slower than the owner's
   read. The test now writes through the permission rules and reads back
   as the backend; the general cost is raised as its own task.
3. **Vine-map positions follow the block's grid**, so a row can start
   well past 1; "vines 1 to 5" could name nothing and still save. The
   form now shows where each row's vines run and refuses such a range,
   with no signal too; the database refuses it as well.
4. **The pick's kg per vine divided the whole weight by the vines of
   the one line that knew them** — the weigh sheets' rule, harmless when
   an unresolved line was a small add-on, absurd once a line can be a
   whole vineyard. It now divides only the lines that know their vines,
   and says how much of the pick that covers.
5. A slow failed load could land after a successful one and show an
   error over a working form; only the latest answer counts now.
6. The pick date defaulted to the UTC date — yesterday, before 1 pm in
   New Zealand.

## Two phone fixes

- **Location help.** Declining location once on an iPhone Home Screen
  app left a message saying "allow it in the browser" — and a Home
  Screen app has no browser. A refusal now gives the way back for the
  device, behind "How to turn it on": Location Services and Safari
  Websites for the Home Screen app and Safari, Chrome's own setting on
  an iPhone, the site's or app's permissions on Android.
- **The clock bar.** A scrolled page showed beneath the phone's clock.
  The page now keeps clear of the areas the phone reserves, with an
  opaque strip under the clock. Checked at phone size in a browser, with
  a simulated clock bar; only a real iPhone can confirm it.

its message from standard input, a guard the Mac's `head` rejects — were
caught on the next check.
