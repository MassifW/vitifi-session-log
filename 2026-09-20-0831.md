# The pergola, press splits, and a draw tool

*Volumes, yields, vine counts, block layouts and input rates are withheld
from this public copy.*

## 1. The pergola

**Why it was missing:** its row in the source sheet leaves the "Named
Range" cell blank and names itself in the Block column instead. The
parser required the named range, so it skipped the row entirely. A shape
drawn over the pergola resolved its vines correctly and then reported no
clone and no rootstock, because there was no planting record for them to
hang on.

Now imported, with its clones, rootstocks, spacing and area. The area is
derived from vine count and vine density rather than read from the header
cell, which held something else — and the derived figure lands exactly on
the sheet's own total row.

**What doesn't fit the planting model**, reported rather than forced:

- It is **pergola-trained**, and the sheet still describes it as rows and
  bays like the VSP blocks. That yields the right vine count and the
  right spacing, and the surveyed row geometry agrees, so it is modelled
  as a planting like any other. Whether "rows" means the same thing
  physically on a pergola is a question for the vineyard, not something
  to settle from a spreadsheet.
- Its **vine count does not reconcile** with the vine table — a handful
  of vines apart. Left as it is.
- Its **dead and young columns together exceed its own total**. In the
  other block those columns are subsets of the count; here they cannot
  both be. Not resolved.

A shape over it now reports its clones and rootstocks like anywhere else.

## 2. Spacing, and two more source-sheet traps

**"DISTANCE BETWEEN" is the distance between POSTS, not vines.** Both
sheets carry a row width, a post-to-post bay length and a vines-per-bay
count. The bay was stored as the vine spacing, so plantings claimed
metres between vines that are a little over a metre apart. Bay length now
has its own column and the vine spacing is derived from it.

Nothing downstream was wrong because of this — vine positions come from
the surveyed row geometry, which always had the right figures — but the
plantings field was there to be read and was wrong by a factor of seven.

**Numeric text.** These sheets mix numbers and numeric *strings* in the
same column — one block's vines-per-bay is the text `'7'` where the next
block's is the number `7`. Reading with a type check dropped every value
stored as text, which was most of them. That is why the previous pass
left the geometry null nearly everywhere.

**One site's row spacing is now its surveyed figure**, in the plantings
table as well as the row geometry.

**One block disagrees with itself and stays disagreeing.** Its sheet
computes a different vine spacing from what the survey says. Both are
recorded facts from different sources and only one can be right. Vine
positions are built from the survey, so nothing downstream changes; a new
view puts the two side by side so the disagreement is visible rather than
living in a migration comment.

## 3. Press splits

Three of forty-five press runs had a fermenter giving more than the
vessels that received it. In each, **every other vessel's line matched
its volume exactly** — it was one line per run carrying more than its
container could hold, the largest by a wide margin.

**Modelled as a split plus a residual.** Each line is scaled to the
volume its vessel actually holds, so no line claims more than its
container; the difference is recorded as a loss against the fermenters
the run came off, apportioned by their own shares of that movement. The
fermenter's total now balances — what left equals what is in vessels plus
what is recorded as gone — and **all forty-five runs reconcile**.

**Categorised `unexplained`, not `lees`.** That much volume off a
two-fermenter press blend is plausible as hard pressings and lees, and it
is not written down anywhere. Calling it lees would be a guess wearing
the clothes of a record. Nothing here reconstructs where it went, because
the records do not say — if it went somewhere rather than being lost,
this is wrong in a way that is visible and correctable rather than
silent.

## 4. The draw tool

Tap-a-vertex polygon drawing on the map, full-screen, phone-first. No
draw plugin — the project has none, and a pick shape needs tap-a-vertex,
undo and clear rather than a toolbar of shape types.

The preview is the point, and it runs before there is anything to save:
vine count, the clone and rootstock split per block, whether the clone is
**exact** or **estimated** on the same basis as a row range, and — in
amber — **every block the shape crosses**, since a blob that quietly
caught the neighbouring block is the failure this guards against. A block
with no planting covering its rows says so rather than showing an empty
split.

A drawn shape satisfies the same requirement a row range does, because it
is an extent in its own right. The shape and the vines it caught are
stored by one function so the two cannot drift.

## Open defect

**A server action returns a 500 when the drawer opens.** The component
renders, the controls work, and both actions it calls return correct
results when called directly against the database — verified. But the
framework logs a JSON parse failure on the server-action transport and
the browser gets a 500. I added a guard for one way in, but I have not
root-caused it and should not claim the drawer works end to end. The
resolution, preview and storage underneath it are tested and correct;
what is unproven is the round trip from this component.
