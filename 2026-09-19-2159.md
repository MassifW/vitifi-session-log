# 2026-09-19 21:59 — The movement ledger and volume reconciliation

*Yields, per-lot volumes and block composition percentages are withheld
from this public copy. The reasoning, the defects found and the design
decisions are all here; only the numbers that would disclose production
volumes are held back.*

## What was asked

Build an explicit movement ledger to replace the litres-per-lot columns
carried over from the spreadsheet, plus a composition function, balance
checks, a reconciliation UI, and tests. Acceptance criteria were given as
specific figures from the 2024 vintage: two lots with implausible
recoveries, a matched pair of barrels to move between them, one blend's
barrel count and composition, and a vintage-level input/output balance.

## The finding that reframed the job

**No 2024 winery data was in the database, and none was in the repo.**
The database holds 2025 only. Of its 823 events, the ones dated 2024 are
all phenology observations — no lots, vessels, picks or pressings.
`data/source/` has only 2025 files on the winery side.

The 2024 data is real, though — it lives in a downloads folder outside
the repo and outside git: a 2024 barrel log and a 2024 weigh sheet /
daily ferment log. Four copies exist; the one in the organised vintage
folder is both newer and the curated one, so it is treated as canonical.
Every acceptance figure reconstructs from it exactly.

I initially reported the opposite — that the 2024 data did not exist
anywhere — on the strength of searching the repo and the database. That
was wrong, and the fan-out search that found it is the reason the rest of
this session is worth anything.

## Reading the 2024 log correctly took three corrections

1. **Rounds are stacked newest-first.** A fresh tasting round is inserted
   at the top of the sheet, so row order runs backwards in time. Reading
   from the bottom inverts every finding — it reports one barrel as
   holding a wine it stopped holding a year ago, which would have made
   the headline defect name the wrong barrel.
2. **"Latest row per barrel code" is not the same as "the latest
   round".** One barrel stops appearing partway down; carrying its
   last-seen row forward counts it twice and inflates the vintage. The
   round boundary is found by walking down from the top and stopping at
   the first code seen twice.
3. **The log maintains its own per-lot totals row**, and there are two
   distinct values across the thirteen of them. They are not in
   conflict: the older figure is the volume at barrel-down, which is
   what recovery measures, and the newer is what is still in barrel a
   year of topping later. The difference is real loss across seven lots.
   The acceptance criterion uses the barrel-down figure, correctly.

## The defects found in the 2024 data

- **Two barrels' volume is booked against the wrong lot.** Both carry
  one lot name in the log's own lot column, but their litres sit in a
  different lot's column. That lot therefore reads as having more wine
  than fruit, while the other reads as nearly empty. Moving the volume
  brings both back into a plausible range. This is exactly the defect
  the brief described.
- **A barrel code was reused mid-log** and now holds a different wine;
  four codes in total were reused this way, and two barrels vanish from
  the log with no bottling or transfer record.
- **The vintage fruit total is not stored anywhere.** It is the ferment
  log deduped by tank — three tanks carry two lot aliases each, a
  substantial double count — less one excluded non-Pinot lot. Both rules
  are now encoded explicitly and printed by the parse script, because
  neither is discoverable from the data.

## What was built

**Schema**: `movement` (one destination vessel, typed, with the free
run / press run / press blend fraction and a supersedes chain) and
`movement_line` (one source per row, lot or vessel, with litres); a
`loss` table with six categories; an `is_assumed` flag on every litres
column; and a constrained `fill_number` column alongside the existing
free-text fill status.

Four of the six requested vessel columns already existed under other
names and were deliberately not renamed — the rename would have churned
four call sites for nothing.

**Backfill**: written as a re-runnable function with a dry-run mode
rather than a straight INSERT. It is lossless rather than reconstructed:
the 2025 pressing import already recorded exactly one positive movement
row per event with negative rows matching the event payload's sources
exactly, and the fraction survives in the event notes. It reads the
imported ledger and writes nothing back to it.

**A correction to my own first attempt**: the initial is_assumed rule
flagged only volumes that both equalled nominal capacity and disagreed
with their sources. Checked afterwards, **every destination volume in
the database equals nominal capacity exactly** — none of them is a
measurement. The flag now covers all of them, and
agreement-with-sources is reported separately as a gap, which is what it
actually is.

**Balance checks**: views for lot-level and vintage-level balance, a
derived vintage (the `lots` table has no vintage column — it comes from
pick dates, inherited through parentage), and a matched-pairs function.

The load-bearing decision is in how litres are attributed. Summing the
ledger by lot gives **every** harvest lot a negative total, because each
one gives its wine away to a differently-named child lot when pressed.
A naive threshold check would flag every lot and find nothing real.
Litres are instead walked back up the parentage chain to the harvest
lots, with proportions normalised — several blends' parentage rows do
not sum to what their vessels actually hold. The total is conserved
exactly.

Matched pairs scores candidate moves against the vintage's own recovery
rather than against the theoretical ceiling, and searches whole-barrel
subsets largest-first. On the 2024 figures that criterion picks the
right number of barrels; scoring against the ceiling alone would have
accepted a wrong answer equally well. It proposes only — there is
deliberately no apply function beside it.

**Composition**: walks a vessel's or a blend's incoming movements back
through lots to picks, returning percentages by lot, block, site and
vintage with a per-dimension `estimated` flag and the reason. Clone and
rootstock return as *unavailable* with an explanation rather than as an
empty result — they are recorded per barrel in one site's barrel log and
have never been imported, and they are not derivable from block, since
one block alone spans two rootstocks. The blend screen now takes its
composition from this function instead of its own volume-prorated
arithmetic.

**UI**: a fourth Cellar view, **Check**, listing anomalies worst-first
with the proposed fix where there is one.

**Tests**: `npm test` via the built-in Node test runner, no new
dependency. 19 tests, all passing. The 2024 acceptance suite asserts
against the parse script's reading of the winery's workbooks — the only
place those numbers exist — and skips itself loudly if the workbooks are
absent. The 2025 suite asserts against the live database. Writing the
2024 suite against the database would have meant either importing a
vintage nobody asked for or asserting figures that can never be
produced.

One acceptance figure needed the brief's own tolerance: two of the
composition percentages round a decimal differently from the log's
precomputed row. The stated tolerance covers it; asserting exact
equality would have been asserting a rounding.

## Left undone, deliberately

- **2024 is not imported.** The schema, checks and tests are ready for
  it, but importing a whole vintage into the production database was not
  in the brief, and several questions block a clean import — including
  whether the misbooked volume should be corrected at import or
  preserved with the correction recorded on top.
- **Clone and rootstock composition** needs its own import pass, and
  only one of the two sites records it at all.
- **The loss table has no rows.** The volume gaps found are surfaced as
  anomalies, not auto-written as losses; writing them would be applying
  a data correction, which the brief explicitly excludes.
- **One site's destination vocabulary** is still missing from the
  controlled list, so a number of its barrels read as undecided when
  they are in fact assigned.
