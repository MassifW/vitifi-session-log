# Vintage separation, and the harvest entry screen

*Yields, volumes, vine counts, block layouts and input rates are withheld
from this public copy.*

## 1. Vintage was never a filter

The cellar read layer contained no reference to vintage at all. The
separation has been accidental, not designed: every cellar screen reads
the older movement table, which holds one vintage's rows only. The
earlier vintage was imported into the new ledger, which those screens do
not read.

So the "whole vintage" blend overview scoped to *whatever happened to be
in that table*.

**The check passes, for the wrong reason.** The blend shows its own
vintage's barrel count, not both vintages added together — but nothing
filtered it. The older vintage is simply invisible to those queries, and
it has no destination-assignment records either, so it could not group
even if the ledger were shared. The moment the cellar is repointed at the
new ledger — which it must be — the other vintage would have appeared
unfiltered.

Fixed: the barrel list, blend overview and blend detail all take a
vintage and filter on the lot's vintage.

## 2. Season and vintage are separate axes

The header's season label is a growing-season label and scopes vineyard
work. The cellar now has its own vintage selector and states the vintage
on the tab header and on the blend overview. Right now the current season
is the *next* one while the cellar holds wine from the two before it —
one label cannot serve both, and the header's was silently implying
otherwise.

## 3. Blend litres vs barrel counts: genuine vessel variation

Not a grouping bug. Every blend reconciles to real vessel capacities —
the estate has standard barriques plus a handful of larger formats and
one egg, and the blends that look "wrong" per barrel are simply the ones
containing a large-format vessel. Each was checked vessel by vessel.

Worth carrying forward: the three largest vessels are already flagged as
carrying an assumed volume, and each is one of the unexplained gaps
already surfaced — the press sheets and the barrel log disagree about
what went into them, and the barrel log records the vessel's rated
capacity.

## 4. The harvest entry screen

The first screen meant to replace a spreadsheet in daily use. Built on
what the weigh sheets actually record, because the crew checks the screen
against the sheet in their hand.

**Settings, not constants.** Pallet tare, crate tare and the planning
factor for predicted volume are the three cells every tank sheet carries
near the top. They are now stored, site-scoped and effective-dated, so a
new crate does not retrospectively change last vintage's net weights.

**Pick lines reference a planting, plus where within it.** A row range is
*required* where the planting spans more than one clone or rootstock
above a materiality threshold, optional elsewhere. The threshold matters:
one planting carries a twenty-vine interplant among a couple of thousand,
and prompting on that trains the crew to ignore the prompt.

**Allocation warns, never blocks.** A line may be split between
fermenters and a lot may draw from several picks across several days. A
mismatch between allocated and picked weight is recorded and surfaced,
not prevented.

**Offline.** Entries queue on the phone, and every draft carries a
client-generated reference that the server is idempotent on — so a save
pressed twice at a weighbridge with no signal lands once. Every storage
read and write is wrapped; if the phone will not store it either, the
screen says so plainly rather than clearing the form.

**Yield per hectare is summed effective area**, not one figure times a
single planting density. A real pick in the source data spans one block
at two different vine densities, and a flat density gets it visibly
wrong.

### Acceptance

**The named acceptance lot does not exist.** Both vintages' workbooks
were searched; the only matches for the name are barrel codes, tasting
notes, and an unrelated label. No lot of that name, and none with the
described shape.

A different real lot was used instead, and is a better test: four lines,
three of them row ranges inside the one block that spans two plantings at
different densities, plus one line with no rows and no vine count. Every
line's tare and net reproduce the sheet exactly, as do the totals, the
vine count and kilograms per vine.

The effective area and yield figures differ by 0.006%, and the difference
is entirely the sheet's own rounding — its area cells divide by a density
rounded to a whole number where the planting data holds the real one.
Asserted to a tolerance covering that rather than matched exactly;
matching exactly would mean reproducing a rounding error on purpose. The
test also redoes the sheet's arithmetic its own way and confirms that is
the whole gap.

Verified live in the browser at phone width.

**Exactness**: a row range inside that block resolves clone with no
apportioning. Rootstock is *not* exact for the same rows, because two
rootstocks are present there — which is right: exactness is per
dimension, not per pick.

**No backfill.** The historical picks are untouched and keep their
estimated provenance. The screen's recent-picks list shows only what was
entered through it and says so.

### Two import bugs the screen exposed

1. **Vine density was missing from a third of the plantings.** The
   vine-info sheet writes a block's geometry once, in a merged cell on
   the row that names the block, and the parser read it literally. Those
   plantings could produce no yield-per-hectare figure at all — the
   screen showed net weight and omitted the yield, exactly the kind of
   silent gap that gets noticed a vintage later.
2. **One block was imported under another block's letter.** Every block
   header in that sheet is written one way except one, which is written
   as a bare letter; the parser only recognised the common form, so that
   block's planting inherited the previous letter.

Both fixed in the parser and repaired in place, with a guard that raises
if any planting is left without a density.

Also fixed: the tab bar had no horizontal overflow, so on a phone the
last tabs could not be reached at all — which matters now that one of
them is meant to be used at a weighbridge.

## Not built

- **The map blob.** Drawing a shape and resolving it to the rows and
  vines it covers is specified, and the schema is ready — the pick line
  stores a polygon alongside the row range, so both the shape and the
  resolved set persist. The drawing UI and the resolution against the
  vine data are not written. Row ranges cover one site's picks entirely;
  the blob is what the other site needs, and it is the next piece.
- **Repointing the cellar at the new ledger.** It still reads the older
  one. Vintage filtering makes that correct for what is there, but two
  live ledgers is not a state to leave alone.
