# 24 Oct 2026: forecasting the stages from other seasons

The question: forecast the growth stages and harvest from the long-term average, from a chosen past season as an analogue year, or from a weighting between them, applied to this season's observed stages, with each basis side by side and the spread shown.

- **How it forecast before.** Each stage is the previous observed stage plus a typical gap; only the budburst to flowering step is counted in heat units, the rest in days, because heat made the later steps worse in testing. The harvest step rests on a single season.
- **What data exists.** The app's own stage records start in 2021, so none exist for the analogue seasons asked about. Open-Meteo's archive does reach back before 2015, but weather alone could only move flowering, and the later stages follow by the same amount.
- **What carried it.** A members' industry publication (VineFacts, New Zealand Winegrowers) prints 50% budburst, 50% flowering and 8 Brix dates for Pinot noir at two Central Otago sites for twelve seasons, including the seasons asked about. The gap between stages in any of them, or the average of all, is applied to this season's observed budburst. A second source, a neighbouring vineyard's temperature file, was read and not used.
- **Built.** A box on the Growth Stages tab, hidden until budburst has a date: the average and three named seasons side by side, a slider between the average and one chosen season, the spread between the rows, and the width over all twelve seasons. It runs on the phone from readings already loaded: no server call, no database change, no new service. Harvest is the app's existing day count from flowering, and says so.
- **Honest limits, on the screen.** One season's gap is that season's weather; twelve seasons is a small sample. The industry record's gap for one site disagrees with the app's own, and four of its printed gaps are not quite the days between the dates beside them; a test names the four.
- **Not built.** Swapping an analogue year's daily heat in for the days to come, and a whole-season heat model for the later stages (to be tested before any build).

## Later the same day: the winery's own seasons replace the placeholders

The industry record's dates are stand-ins. Once a vineyard has three finished seasons with both 50% dates from its own readings, the box adds an own-average row and lets the record be switched to the winery's own seasons. The app records no 8 Brix date, so that one gap stays borrowed and the row says so. A neighbouring vineyard's stage dates and temperatures are kept as further placeholders with its name left off. Checked on made-up seasons at phone width; not yet on live readings.

## Later: weather swapped in, and a heat test

The winery's own seasons now start the box once a vineyard has three. A section opened on request counts flowering in heat units instead of days, with the weather to come taken from the average of earlier seasons or from a chosen season, and an option to count the 8 Brix step by heat too. Tried first on twelve seasons of the industry record, leaving each out: heat was further out than days for both steps at both sites (flowering 9.2 and 8.7 days against 5.4 and 4.9; 8 Brix 12.6 and 8.4 against 4.5 and 4.3), even knowing the season's real weather. So it is shown as another opinion, with those numbers beside it, not as the better one. A whole-season heat model was not built for that reason; the vineyard's own weather logger, when it arrives, is the next test. Not released.
