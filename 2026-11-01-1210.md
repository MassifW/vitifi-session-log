# 1 Nov 2026: a winery's own Map icons, and hiding groups of icons on the Map and the safety map print

**Asked (Mike):**
1. Could a user make their own icons for the Map's menu? Of three ways (pick a drawing from a library, a pixel editor, upload a picture) he chose picking from a library.
2. An option to hide groups of icons, above all for the health and safety maps, where a crowded map can hide what matters.

## 1. A winery's own icons (on a branch; the database change not yet on live)

- **"Make your own"** sits at the end of each group of the icon menu. Someone searches the Lucide drawings (about 1,600; the set the app's buttons use, ISC licence) by a word, names one and puts it in a group. A safety icon also gets its ISO 7010 sign shape (safe condition, fire equipment, warning, read this, facilities). It joins the menu for everyone at that winery.
- **Kept per winery.** A new table belongs to the winery, behind the row-level security wall every winery's records sit behind, and a point can only show its own winery's icons.
  - The drawing is stored as SVG path text. The server copies it from the library by name, and the database checks every character, since it is drawn into the page.
  - One in Safety is a safety icon: made and changed only by those who keep the health and safety documents, the same rule as the built-in safety icons.
  - An icon still on the map cannot be deleted; the database says how many places still show it.
- **Drawn everywhere the built-in icons are:** the map, the key, the menu and the printed safety map, where one on the warning triangle is listed with the hazards. It keeps its line drawing in the pixel style, because a 16-pixel copy of a line drawing doesn't read.
- **The library** is a generated file, about 70 KB compressed, fetched only when the form opens. A test fails if it drifts from the icon package's installed version.
- **Decided without asking:** "is it a safety icon" is its group, as for the built-in icons, not a separate tick.
- **Found and fixed:** the delete control is a form of its own. Placed inside the icon form, its submit would also have saved the form.

## 2. Hiding groups of icons (on a branch; no database change)

- **The printed safety map** has a tick for each group the vineyard has icons of. It starts from what the Map hides. **Safety is always on**: it is the safety map, and its emergency panel comes from those icons.
- **On the Map**, hidden groups are remembered per phone, and the card names them, so a cleared map is never read as an empty one.
- **One rule for both:** a hidden group's icon comes off a shared spot (a shed's first aid kit still shows with Buildings hidden), and a spot left with nothing isn't drawn.
- **Found and fixed:** an apostrophe in the manual's new text broke its string; the tests caught it before the commit.

## Checked

- **Gates.** All passed on both branches: tests, typecheck and build.
- **Local copy.** A local copy of the nightly backup, in its own Docker project. `psql` isn't installed here, so a small wrapper ran it inside the copy's database container. The backup predated that morning's migrations, which were applied to the copy first.
- **Database harness.** On the copy, the new table's checks passed 35 of 35, and the existing points' checks 26 of 26. Every export view survived a backup round-trip.
- **Browser, hiding.** As an owner and as a vineyard hand:
  - Hiding two groups took 14 spots down to 11. Spots shared with an assembly area kept it.
  - The choice survived a reload, and the print started from it with Safety locked on.
  - The printed key left the hidden group out.
- **Browser, own icons.**
  - The hand made a water icon and placed it. They were not offered Safety.
  - The owner made a safety icon on the warning triangle, and the print listed it as a hazard and in the key.
  - A delete of an icon still on the map was refused in the database's words.
- **Errors seen, not from this work.** The machine was overloaded (two local copies running), and some calendar reads timed out. A stale sign-in cookie and the missing weather function also logged errors before the migrations were applied.
