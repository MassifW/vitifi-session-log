# 24 Oct 2026: the vine sheets, and drone layers trimmed to the blocks

Mike asked whether the per-vine spreadsheets (one cell per vine, "multiple layers of data") could be brought into the app "as spreadsheets or something with a similar appearance/function", then for the same data as map layers (clones, rootstocks, vine age, irrigation) and a choice to show a drone layer whole or trimmed to the drawn blocks. Later in the session he decided the Google sheets will no longer be used: the vine data is kept in VITIFI only, and the backups must be able to rebuild working spreadsheets.

- **What the sheets hold.** Read with openpyxl, every layer of a cell, not only what the August parser kept. A cell is free text: variety, clone, rootstock, nursery, planting year, young or not productive, irrigation zone, and on some blocks dripper flow, soil at planting, stock type, transplant and from where, hand watering, layering from the neighbouring vine, planned layering, a found planting tag, and dated sightings such as powdery mildew. The colours are conditional-formatting rules on that text (a clone-by-rootstock palette with status colours overriding it, fill the same as the font so the text is hidden until a cell is selected). With no validation, the words decide the colour and the counts, and spelling drift changes both. The summary sheets are COUNTIF formulas over the text, and the yield workbooks read their productive-vine counts from them. One block's sheet is copied at each season's end; a replanted vine's cell is overwritten, so only the copy keeps the old vine.
- **What the app holds.** Each living vine's address (block, row, position, bay, and a derived point on the map), and nothing else of the sheets per vine. No screen shows a vine.
- **Found: vine positions probably shifted along some rows, not yet checked against the live database.** The function that places vines measures from where each row's anchor was clicked, but a vine's position number counts the sheet's blank cells before the row's first vine, and the anchors were drawn on the real rows. Where a row starts with blanks, its vines would sit too far along it, by tens of metres in the worst rows. Saving a block's rows does not recompute positions either. To be checked on a restored copy before any vine layer goes on the map.
- **Designed, not built.** Three independent designs (the record, the screen, the map) and a critic that merged them. A Vines tab laid out like the sheets (drawn on a canvas, coloured by any layer, a key that counts, tap a vine, select many); recording jobs in the row, offline; each vine space holding a record per physical vine and dated records of what happened, so a real change keeps its history and a correction is edited in place with no trace; vine layers on the map once placement is corrected; and a nightly workbook laid out like the old sheets (a tab per block, the vine's details as text, colours as rules, count formulas) that works as a spreadsheet again. Order: facts on a restored copy, a read-only tab from a fresh export (the sheets still the master), then recording and the switch-over, mid-December at the earliest. A list of questions for Mike about the sheets' own words and marks.
- **A clickable mockup** from the parsed sheets, kept private (it carries block layouts and vine counts): colour by any layer, the sheet's own colour rules reproduced exactly, tap a vine for its card and what that place held each season, select a patch and see the harvest ranges.
- **Built: trim to the blocks.** "Whole image" / "Blocks only" under a visible picture layer. Each Leaflet zoom level of the layer gets the block outlines as a CSS clip path in its own pixels (through `_onCreateLevel`), so the clip scales with the picture through a pinch; outlines traced along the outer rows are widened by half a row (`effective_boundary()`), so the outer rows' canopy stays; tiles with no block under them are not fetched. Kept per phone for now; "for everyone" waits on a database change and Mike's yes.
- **Fixed alongside.** The swipe comparison hid both layers (it set `inset(%)` on a Leaflet pane, which has no size); now a polygon in the pane's own pixels, redrawn as the map moves, vectors included. Vector and picture layers could share a stacking place. A refused layer-display change was never noticed (the change showed, then went back); now it is said, and logins that cannot change it for everyone change only their own phone's map. Block outlines, photo pins and the "where I am" dot sat under every drone layer; now above it. Base maps vanished past zoom 19 when a drone layer let the map zoom further; now stretched.
- **Checked.** Twelve tests of the clip and swipe arithmetic and of the Leaflet internals the trim relies on; a test page with Leaflet 1.9.4 and the compiled clip code through a pan, an animated zoom (a frame taken mid-animation), a zoom past the layer's own detail, swipe with trim, and back; the widened outlines read from the database and parsed; the project's guard tests. **Not checked:** the Map tab itself signed in, and a pinch on an iPhone.
- **Reviewed.** Three independent reviewers and a skeptic. Fixed: a reorder by a login that cannot change layers for everyone snapped back while the panel said it stayed on the phone; an old kept copy of the map would have trimmed at the drawn line without saying so; a stale stacking order for a vector layer still loading.
- **Gates.** The full suite passes but for one database check of the visitor QR page, which expects only its own test document and fails on live data this change does not touch (logged). Typecheck and build pass.
- **Merged.** On Mike's yes, merged to main; not released yet.
